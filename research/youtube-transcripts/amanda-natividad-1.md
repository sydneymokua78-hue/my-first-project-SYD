Why “zero-click marketing” works as a phrase — and why naming matters



The worst thing you can name is something that already has a name. If you try to rename something people already understand, it falls flat. Zero-click marketing works because it named something people already saw happening but didn’t have a term for.



Rand Fishkin also explored similar ideas around “marketing to sources of influence.”



Why zero-click marketing is effective but hard to sell internally



It’s easy to agree with zero-click marketing in theory, but hard to sell internally because leadership wants clear attribution. But marketing is not direct response anymore.



The rise and limits of software-based attribution



We used to have detailed attribution dashboards showing full customer journeys, but over time we lost that clarity and over-relied on simplified models like first-click or last-click attribution.



How Brendan talks to leaders about marketing impact beyond hard attribution



Tools like DreamData help map full buyer journeys across touchpoints like ads, emails, webinars, and outbound. You can see patterns like newsletter subscribers converting faster and closing deals faster.



But attribution is still messy. People don’t buy from one touchpoint—they buy when they’re ready.



Why people buy when they’re ready — and why attribution gets messy



I got a DM from someone who said multiple people had recommended me over time before they became a lead. That’s not traceable in attribution.



Even small purchases are influenced by many unseen touchpoints like podcasts, ads, emails, and conversations.



My take: make it easy for people to choose you when they’re ready



The best strategy is to be clear, consistent, and easy to understand so when people are ready, you’re their obvious choice.



“Don’t be the best. Be their favorite.”



Being the favorite matters more than being the best. People in organizations often push deals forward just because someone internally likes a brand. That influence is not measurable but very real.



Brendan’s zero-click ask: follow his work and learn along the way



The best clients are those who already consume your content. The goal is to become their favorite through consistent presence on LinkedIn and newsletters.



Brendan’s practical advice for getting started with zero-click measurement



Ask people how they heard about you at multiple points:

\- when they convert

\- when they start or finish a trial

\- when they talk to sales



Use open-ended answers and analyze them with AI tools. This gives better insight than dropdown attribution models.
