Hi everyone and welcome to GEO AEO LLMO separating fact from fiction and how to win in an AI search. This is an online adapted version of the presentation that I gave at Mozcon New York City last week and I just wanted to share it online as well.



So for anybody who doesn't know me, my name is Lily Ray and I'm the VP of SEO strategy and research at an agency called Amsive based here in New York City. This was my fifth annual year presenting at Mozcon, the first ever Mozcon New York City and my ninth year in a row attending Mozcon.



Now for anybody in the SEO and AI search space, this is a bit of what it's felt like to stay on top of all the latest trends and developments this year. We have all types of new vocabulary and terminology that we need to learn like chunking content, prompt engineering, token efficiency, cosine similarity, model context protocol, agentic workflows—so much new concepts and things to learn.



And I would say the most important question on everyone’s mind is: is this GEO, AEO, LLMO, or really just SEO?



To present this, I wanted to keep things simple with a story. I thought back to this timeless article describing our industry as one giant alligator party. So I thought I would stick with that motif.



Believe it or not, in New York City we have had alligators appearing in subway systems, though you’re more likely to encounter a rat.



Our story begins in mid 2023 in the SEO swamp where SEO industry evolutions are handled by a party of alligators.



Once a year, they gather for the SEO hype cycle monopoly tournament where each insists they are winning but no one lands on page one.



We meet Mother SEO, strong and tired of explaining canonical tags, expecting another “next big thing.” Father PPC is always reallocating budget toward anything measurable.



Each alligator represents an SEO era:

\- Mobile update forced responsive design

\- Voice search once promised a future of spoken queries

\- AMP promised speed and dominance but faded

\- Featured snippets became the overachiever of zero-click search

\- Core Web Vitals became the gym bro obsessed with performance scores



The SEO swamp lives in harmony, but a new evolution is about to hatch.



An egg appears—golden. It whispers: “Feed me data.”



The family debates its name:

AEO, LLMO, answer engine optimization… until grandma SEO says: “This is all just SEO.”



Father PPC names it GEO: Generative Engine Optimization.



Immediately, media declares SEO is dead and GEO has arrived.



Rumors spread:

\- People start on ChatGPT instead of Google

\- Content must be chunked and embedded

\- Cosine similarity matters

\- You must optimize for AI ingestion

\- Reddit, YouTube, and Facebook citations matter

\- AI search is multimodal

\- Query fanout is essential



GEO becomes a celebrity, dominating conversations and dashboards.



But reality begins to show:

AI overviews reduce clicks, impressions rise while clicks drop, but SEO KPIs are still evolving.



New tracking tools appear everywhere.



Publishers scramble for visibility, while GEO takes credit for everything.



But underneath, nothing fundamentally new is happening.



We’ve seen this before.



Chunking is just formatting.

FAQs and structured data already existed.

Multimodal content already existed through YouTube, podcasts, and social media.

Digital PR already handled brand mentions.

Query fanout is just keyword research and “People Also Ask.”



AI search grows—but Google is still dominant.



Most LLM traffic is only 1–2% compared to organic search.



Most ChatGPT users still use Google, often more than before.



AI search is expansionary, not replacement.



LLMs are used for:

\- Writing

\- Coding

\- Brainstorming

\- Learning



Google is still used for:

\- Fact checking

\- Navigation

\- Shopping

\- Maps

\- Search intent queries



GEO eventually realizes it is not a replacement, but an evolution of SEO.



The foundation remains:

Search engines retrieve, index, and ground AI responses.



LLMs rely on retrieval augmented generation (RAG), meaning search engines power AI answers.



Every LLM citation ultimately comes from search engines like Google or Bing.



Many so-called GEO tactics are just SEO fundamentals:

\- Clear content structure

\- FAQs

\- Schema markup

\- Content quality

\- Digital PR

\- Brand authority



Even Google confirms:

Good SEO is good GEO.



Key takeaway:

AI search visibility is earned through strong SEO fundamentals.



What changes are metrics:

\- Brand impressions

\- AI visibility share

\- Topic-level presence

\- Engagement from AI traffic

\- Citation frequency across LLMs



Tracking tools help measure this.



You should also:

\- Answer branded questions

\- Build content for E-E-A-T

\- Expand FAQ content

\- Improve authority pages

\- Localize content

\- Use real user-generated content

\- Build digital PR presence



Reddit, YouTube, and Wikipedia strongly influence LLM outputs.



AI search success depends on being visible everywhere, not just in one system.



Eventually GEO realizes:

It is not replacing SEO—it is just part of SEO’s evolution.



And the SEO swamp continues, adapting as always.



Thank you for joining the SEO swamp.
